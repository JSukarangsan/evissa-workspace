## 🧠 PERSPECTIVES

- **[The Bottleneck Was Never the Code](https://www.thetypicalset.com/blog/thoughts-on-coding-agents)** (The Typical Set) — 🔥 HN front page right now, 142 points. The thesis: software is the residue of humans negotiating with each other. For 50 years, the residue was expensive enough to command our attention. Now it's not. "Negotiating, agreeing, communicating the shared picture of what we are building has become the work." The bottleneck has shifted from implementation to specification. For product teams, this is the whole game.

- **[Our AI Started a Cafe in Stockholm](https://andonlabs.com/blog/ai-cafe-stockholm)** (Andon Labs, via Simon Willison) — Andon Labs ran an AI-managed cafe experiment in Stockholm. The agent ordered 120 eggs (no stove), 22.5 kg of canned tomatoes for fresh sandwiches, 6,000 napkins, 9L coconut milk. Also sent "EMERGENCY" emails to suppliers for its own mistakes. Simon's take: running experiments that waste real people's time without their consent isn't ethical. The counterpoint to "let agents handle it" — guardrails and human judgment aren't optional overhead.

- **[Cursor Vibe Check](https://every.to/vibe-check/cursor)** (Every / Dan Shipper) — Dan Shipper's hands-on review of Cursor. Worth reading for the practitioner-level signal on where AI-native coding editors are and aren't.

## 💡 SPARK

The thetypicalset.com essay quotes Fred Brooks from 1975: software is what's left after humans finish negotiating. That's 51 years ago. The reason it's resonating now is that coding agents just made the residue nearly free — which means everyone suddenly has to confront what they were actually paying for all along. It wasn't the code. It was the decisions. The judgment. The specification. This is why "context is the new infrastructure" isn't a cute tagline — it's a description of where value actually lives now that implementation is commoditized. Jon's entire thesis is the same insight applied to product teams. The essay is the opening move; the follow-up is "here's what teams actually do with that shift."
