## 🧠 PERSPECTIVES

- **[The Folder Is the Agent](https://every.to/source-code/the-folder-is-the-agent)** (Every / Kieran Klaassen) — 🔥 Solo engineer at Every spent 3 months trying to make agent swarms work. Conclusion: more agents didn't mean faster output. What worked instead — a folder-based structure where each agent has a clear task, bounded context, and human direction. The insight: "the swarm's biggest problem was coordination overhead." Structure beats emergent chaos every time.

- **[How We Run a 25-person Company on Four AI Agents](https://every.to/source-code/how-we-run-a-25-person-company-on-four-ai-agents)** (Every / Brandon Gell) — Every's COO replaced manual cross-referencing of launch calendars, strategy docs, and task lists with Anton, a Notion-based agent in Slack. Four agents now automate the logistics layer of a 25-person company. Key takeaway: "Your Notion is your agent's brain" — interconnected databases make agents powerful, siloed data makes them dumb.

- **[Every's Head of Consulting Just Automated Her Job](https://every.to/podcast/everys-head-of-consulting-just-automated-her-job)** (Every AI & I Podcast / Natalia Quintero) — After speaking to 100+ companies about AI adoption, Quintero's observation: the biggest leaps come from organizations that give people time and space to experiment, not the ones with the fanciest tools or most resources. Mirrors the pattern Jon sees: governance and time-to-experiment beat tool investment.

- **[Building a Feature on Claude Code for Web — From a Phone, While Camping](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/2/sightings/)** (Simon Willison) — Willison built and shipped a full iNaturalist sightings syndication feature for his blog using Claude Code for web, entirely on his phone while camping. Back-populated a decade of content. The model for "always-on building" is arriving faster than expected.

- **[The Rise of Agent-Orchestration Over Code Editing](https://every.to/vibe-check/cursor)** (Every) — Cursor's pivot signals something broader: the dominant coding paradigm has shifted from pair-programming (requires an editor) to agent delegation (doesn't). Products built for the old paradigm are now scrambling. Teams that already moved to orchestration-first workflows are a full generation ahead.

## 💡 SPARK

Symphony's insight was that the bottleneck wasn't model quality — it was human context-switching overhead managing agent sessions. So they removed humans from the loop entirely (except for review) and let agents run continuously against the task board. Every's COO had the same realization: the constraint was the COO's time routing work, not the work itself. Both teams solved it the same way — make the system self-routing, with humans reviewing outputs instead of managing process. That's not just a productivity trick. That's what AI operations actually means at the team level. Jon's been describing this for months. Now there are two concrete case studies with numbers.
