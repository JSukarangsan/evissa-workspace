Figma dropped 7% after Anthropic launched Claude Design last week.

But the threat isn't what most people think it is.

Claude Design doesn't replace Figma for trained designers. Not even close. Direct manipulation, multiplayer collaboration, design system governance at scale. Figma still wins all of that.

The problem is who else was paying for Figma.

Think about your org's Figma seats. How many are actual designers vs PMs viewing files, devs exporting assets, marketers grabbing screenshots? At most companies I've worked with, non-designer seats outnumber designer seats 3 to 1. Sometimes 5 to 1.

That's a LOT of ARR that has nothing to do with design craft.

One HN commenter nailed it: "A lot of their ARR was coming from extra seats for PMs, developers, etc. to view and export and do commenting on the files. I think a lot of this just won't happen on Figma as much."

Now a PM can generate an on-brand interactive prototype through a conversation. A founder can build a pitch deck that actually respects the brand system without opening Figma. A marketer can produce a landing page without filing a design request.

None of these people needed Figma's power. They needed access to what lived inside Figma.

That access just got unbundled.

This is the pattern that kills enterprise software companies. The incumbent owns the professional workflow. The disruptor doesn't compete on that axis. Instead it makes the adjacent users self-sufficient. And those adjacent users were quietly subsidizing the whole business.

Figma's "tool for everyone" seat expansion strategy becomes the vulnerability when AI makes the tool unnecessary for casual users.

The designers are fine. Figma's non-designer revenue is the exposed flank.

You tracking your org's Figma seat breakdown? Curious what the ratio looks like.
