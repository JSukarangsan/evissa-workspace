A MIT study tracked what happens when workers with different skill levels get access to AI tools.

The performance gap got wider. Not smaller.

High-judgment workers got significantly better. Lower-judgment workers didn't improve as much - and in some cases, their output got harder to detect as flawed because AI masked the errors with polish.

This is the part nobody talks about in enterprise AI rollouts.

We spend all this time picking tools and writing prompts. We spend almost no time asking: can this team actually evaluate what the AI gives back?

🧠 The bottleneck moves

In every implementation I've run, there's a predictable pattern. The constraint stops being production (we can all generate more output now) and becomes evaluation.

Can your team tell a good AI output from a bad one? Not in theory. In practice, under deadline, with confidence.

That's the real AI readiness question. Not "what tools do you use?" but "what's your evaluation layer?"

Teams that can't answer that aren't getting leverage. They're accumulating undetected risk at scale.

What are you doing to build evaluation muscle on your team?
