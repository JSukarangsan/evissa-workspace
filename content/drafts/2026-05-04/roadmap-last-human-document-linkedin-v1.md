Talked to a PM last week who said he doesn't write anything anymore.

Specs: AI. Status updates: AI. Meeting summaries: AI. Customer research synthesis: AI.

Then he paused.

"Except the roadmap."

That one stayed with me. Because he's right, and it tells you everything about what AI actually changes for product work.

The roadmap isn't a document. It's a set of bets about what matters, in what order, and why. It encodes organizational values, stakeholder context, and strategic judgment that can't be extracted from a prompt.

If an AI can write your roadmap, you haven't done the real work of strategy yet.

This isn't a limitation of the technology. It's a useful forcing function. All the mechanical writing work - the stuff that drained attention without requiring real thought - is gone. What's left is pure judgment work.

That's a promotion, not a threat.

The question worth asking: what's your version of the roadmap? What in your role can ONLY be decided by you because it requires synthesis of things only you know?

That's where your leverage is.
