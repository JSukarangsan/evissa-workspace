A MIT study tracked what happens when workers with different skill levels get access to AI tools.

The performance gap got wider. Not smaller.

That finding is uncomfortable. But the reason why is what actually matters.

AI shortcuts the reps that build judgment. And you need judgment to evaluate AI. So teams that handed workflows to AI before developing real expertise in those areas are now in a loop - they can't tell good from bad because they never did the work that teaches you the difference.

The output still looks polished. Nobody flags it. The errors compound quietly.

This is what I'd call judgment debt. And most teams don't know they have it.

🧠 What to do about it

The fix isn't to stop using AI. It's to audit where you're using it.

For every workflow your team has handed to AI, ask one question: if this output was subtly wrong, would anyone catch it?

If the answer is no - you have judgment debt in that area. AI arrived before expertise did.

The teams getting exponential returns from AI right now are the ones who built domain depth the hard way first. They argued over decisions. They shipped things and saw what failed. They developed taste. Now AI accelerates everything they already know how to do.

The teams accumulating invisible risk are the ones who outsourced the thinking before they built the judgment to audit it.

Protect the reps that matter. Even when AI could do it faster.

What workflows has your team handed over before you were really ready?
