Cam Worboys from Cash App said something in a Dive Club interview that I keep coming back to.

It's not about speed. It's about reps.

I've been watching this play out in real time with a design team running an AI prototyping pilot. Same tools, same training, same access. Two completely different outcomes.

Some designers ship 12 iterations by lunch. Others spend that same time perfecting their first prompt.

The difference isn't skill. It's tolerance.

The designers struggling aren't the ones who can't use the tools. They're the ones whose identity is still wrapped up in getting it right on the first pass. Precision on draft one. Polish before sharing. That careful, linear process designers have relied on for years.

Here's the thing nobody says out loud: that linear process was never really about quality. It was about risk management. It protected you from looking wrong in front of stakeholders. Every checkpoint, every gate, every "let me refine this before I show anyone" was a shield.

AI made being wrong on the first pass almost free.

And that broke the shield.

The designers who get this are treating AI like a volume game. Draft 1 is supposed to be wrong. So is draft 4. The goal is to get to draft 12 fast enough that draft 12 is actually worth showing. The quality isn't lower. It's higher. Because you explored 12 directions instead of polishing one.

🎯 What this means for design leaders

This isn't a training problem. You don't need another AI tools workshop. You need a coaching conversation about what "good work" looks like now.

If your designers' performance identity is built on first-pass precision, that's what you're actually changing. Not their tooling. Their relationship with imperfection.

Build a culture of fast imperfection, not a culture of AI tools. The reps follow.
