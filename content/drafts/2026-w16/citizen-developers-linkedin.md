Every company I work with has the same hidden pattern: someone in ops or marketing quietly built an AI workflow that saves the team 30+ hours a week. Leadership doesn't know it exists.

These are your citizen developers. And most companies are trying to govern them out of existence before they even get started.

Here's what I keep seeing. A company gets nervous about AI sprawl. Reasonable. So they lead with governance. CoE tiers, graduation criteria, compliance frameworks, approved vendor lists. By the time the policy doc is finished, the person who built that workflow has either stopped building or left for somewhere that lets them.

Governance isn't wrong. The sequence is wrong.

🔧 What actually works

The companies getting this right follow a specific order:

Permission first. Make it culturally OK to build things outside your job description. If your account manager built a client reporting tool with AI, celebrate that publicly. Don't send them to IT for a security review as a first response.

Then experimentation. Give people sandboxed environments. Let them break things where it doesn't matter. Personal productivity tools, team-level automations, stuff that touches no production data. Low stakes, high learning.

Then patterns. Watch what emerges. When 3 teams independently build similar workflows, that's a signal, not a problem.

Then governance. Now you layer in the tiers. Now the graduation criteria make sense because you're governing things that actually exist, not hypothetical risks.

🎯 Why this matters for non-tech companies

If you're a $50M services company, you don't have a 200-person engineering team. You never will. But you have a marketing ops person who automated half their reporting, a project manager who built an AI-powered status tracker, an account lead who created a client brief generator.

Those people are your technological advantage. They understand the business context no engineer ever will. AI just gave them the ability to act on it.

Treat them accordingly.

💡 The real question isn't "how do we control citizen developers." It's "how do we make more of them."
