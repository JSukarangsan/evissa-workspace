I've worked with FAANG. I've worked with startups. The most sophisticated AI operations I've seen? A 200-person company with no AI team.

Not a fluke. I keep seeing this.

Big tech has massive AI investment but the thinking is either locked behind NDAs or diluted across 14 layers of management. By the time an AI workflow gets approved, the model it was designed for is two generations old. The politics alone add 6 months to any implementation.

Startups are bold but narrow. They're solving their problem with AI, not building transferable playbooks. A 12-person team with a clever Claude integration isn't building AI operations. They're hacking.

Growth-stage companies ($10M to $500M) are the sweet spot. Big enough to have real operational complexity. Small enough that one team's AI wins propagate to every other team within weeks, not quarters.

I was in a room last month with a product org of maybe 80 people. They had context infrastructure that would make a Fortune 500 AI team jealous. Not because they had bigger budgets. Because they had no AI team to hand things off to. They had to figure it out themselves, inside the actual work.

That's the difference. FAANG announces AI strategy. Startups demo AI features. Growth-stage companies build the actual operational discipline because nobody else is going to do it for them.

The best case studies in AI operations don't exist yet. The companies doing the most interesting work don't have comms teams writing blog posts about it. They're too busy shipping.

You have to be in the room to see the patterns.

And right now, the room is at growth stage.
