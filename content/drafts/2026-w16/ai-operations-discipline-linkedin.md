I've watched 50+ product teams adopt AI this year.

The ones getting real returns don't have better tools. They have better operations.

Not "operations" like a department or a software category. Operations like a discipline. The way DevOps gave shape to infrastructure chaos in the 2010s, AI operations gives shape to the current mess of disconnected tools, inconsistent workflows, and teams where every person has invented their own AI stack.

Here's what I keep seeing. A product team with 15 people is running Copilot, Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, v0, and maybe three other tools nobody's tracking. Each person has their own prompts, their own context, their own way of working. Zero shared infrastructure. Zero shared learning.

They're all "using AI." None of them have AI operations.

The gap between "we use AI" and "AI makes us better" is entirely operational. It's not about which model you pick. It's about who owns the context layer. Who's making sure the AI tools actually talk to each other. Who's capturing what works and spreading it across the team. Who's measuring whether any of this is actually compounding or just adding noise.

The teams getting 10x returns have someone (or some system) answering those questions. The teams getting 1.5x don't. The tools are identical. The discipline is different.

Most product leaders I talk to are still treating AI adoption as a procurement decision. Pick the right tool, roll it out, move on. That's like saying DevOps is "picking the right CI/CD tool." It misses the entire operational layer that makes the tool useful.

AI operations isn't a product. It's a practice. And almost nobody's defining it yet.

What's your team's AI operational layer look like right now? Structured, or everyone freelancing?
