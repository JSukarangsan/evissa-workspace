A designer on one of my client teams uses four different AI tools on any given day.

Figma for structure. Midjourney for inspiration. v0 for rapid prototyping. Claude for critique loops. She's a power user in one, a complete beginner in another, and actively avoiding a third because it doesn't fit how she thinks.

Her org's official policy is one approved tool.

You can guess which three are "shadow IT."

This is the part most AI adoption programs get wrong. They're designed around a fantasy: that roles are homogeneous. That all designers need the same thing. That one training program reaches everyone at the same level.

The reality from working across multiple teams is messier. In any given team, roughly 50-65% are cautious (not resistant, just waiting for the right fit). 25-35% are motivated but blocked by the wrong tool or wrong starting point. And 5-15% are already running circles around the official stack, building workflows nobody sanctioned.

One rollout can't reach all three groups. The Engaged cohort already solved optionality for themselves. The Cautious cohort needs a completely different path in.

The cross-role differences are easy to see. PMs need different tools than engineers. Obviously. But within-role diversity is the real problem. Same title, same team, completely different workflows and proficiency levels. That's invisible until you look for it.

So what actually works?

Not a 10-person Center of Excellence. One person or one small team holding the map of what's working across the org. Enabling multiple pathways with guardrails. Governance as enablement, not control.

Shadow IT is your org telling you something true. The authorized tooling doesn't fit the actual texture of the work. Instead of auditing who's breaking the rules, ask why the unauthorized tool fits better.

Curious if others are seeing this same pattern. The orgs trying hardest to standardize seem to be the ones with the most underground proliferation.
