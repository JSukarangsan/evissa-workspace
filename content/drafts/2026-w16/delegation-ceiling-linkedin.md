Nobody's talking about the delegation ceiling. Not in the way that matters.

It's not about whether AI can handle longer tasks. That's already happening. METR's research shows autonomous AI task duration roughly doubling every 5 months. The interesting question is whether your team has the configuration layer in place to actually hand things off as that ceiling rises.

Here's how the tiers break down:

🔹 5 minutes - convenience tier
AI writes a function, summarizes a doc, drafts an email. Nice. Incremental. This is where most teams are stuck.

🔹 30 minutes - analyst tier
AI researches a topic, compares options, produces a first-pass recommendation. Now you're saving real cycles. But you need decent context for it to work.

🔹 4-5 hours - workforce multiplier
AI runs a full investigation, builds a prototype, writes and tests a feature. This requires serious context infrastructure. System instructions, codebase awareness, tool integrations. Without that, the AI flails at minute 20.

🔹 Multi-day - structural advantage
AI handles projects. Not tasks, projects. Different org structure entirely. The teams operating here look nothing like the ones stuck at 5 minutes.

Here's what's interesting. The teams investing in the configuration layer right now (context files, MCP integrations, CLAUDE.md, proper system instructions) aren't just getting better outputs today. They're buying options on every future doubling.

Same pattern as websites in 1998. The orgs that built early didn't just have a website. They learned how to think digitally before the stakes were high enough for everyone else to pay attention. By 2004, that learning curve gap almost never closed.

The delegation ceiling is rising whether you're ready or not. The configuration layer is what determines whether your team captures each jump or watches it pass.

Most teams are optimizing for the convenience tier while the ceiling is already moving past analyst and toward workforce multiplier.
