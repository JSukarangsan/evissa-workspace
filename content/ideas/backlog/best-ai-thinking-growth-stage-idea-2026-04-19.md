## Key Concepts
- FAANG has massive AI investment but the thinking is either hidden behind NDAs or diluted across huge orgs — too many layers, too much politics, too slow to share patterns
- Startups have bold thinking but it's siloed — small teams, narrow use cases, no cross-pollination. They're solving *their* problem, not building transferable playbooks
- Growth-stage companies ($10M–$500M) are the sweet spot: big enough to have real operational complexity, small enough that one team's AI wins propagate fast. The thinking compounds because the org is still connected
- This maps directly to Jon's ICP — product teams at mid-market companies are where AI operations actually gets figured out, not where it gets announced

## Content Angles
- Angle 1: *The innovation sweet spot.* FAANG publishes papers. Startups ship demos. Growth-stage companies build the actual operational playbooks because they have to — no AI team to hand it off to, no luxury of a 2-year roadmap
- Angle 2: *Why the best case studies don't exist yet.* The companies doing the most interesting AI work don't have comms teams writing blog posts about it. They're too busy shipping. That's why you have to be in the room to see the patterns
- Angle 3: *Contrarian take on "enterprise AI."* The industry frames AI adoption as an enterprise problem. But enterprise is where AI goes to get committee'd to death. The real discipline is being invented at growth stage

## Possible Hooks
- Hook 1 (direct): "The best AI thinking isn't happening at Google or OpenAI. It's happening at companies you've never heard of."
