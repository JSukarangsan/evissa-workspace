## Key Concepts
- Agent harnesses are the operational layer that sits between raw AI models and useful agent behavior
- This isn't a new category — it's the natural evolution of context engineering. First we learned to feed models better context, now we're learning to constrain and direct agent behavior
- The pattern: prompt engineering → context engineering → agent harnesses. Each layer adds more operational structure around the same core problem: making AI reliably useful

## Content Angles
- Angle 1: The evolutionary arc — context engineering was about input, harnesses are about behavior. Map the progression and show where it's heading
- Angle 2: Harnesses as governance — this is actually an AI operations problem disguised as an infrastructure trend. Product teams need harnesses because they need control, not because they need another abstraction layer
- Angle 3: The contrarian take — most harness discourse is developer-focused ("look at this cool framework"). The real story is organizational: who decides what the harness allows? That's an ops question, not an engineering question

## Landscape Research (April 2026)
- The narrative is real and growing. "If 2025 was the year of the agent, 2026 is the year of the harness" is becoming consensus.
- Framing in the wild: Agent harness = the control plane that wraps around an AI agent's execution. Governs lifecycle, context window, tool access, safety boundaries. Distinct from agent frameworks (LangChain, CrewAI) which help you build agents — harnesses control them.
- Analogy everyone's using: Containers → Kubernetes :: Agents → Harnesses. The build-vs-operate split.
- CNCF's Four Pillars of Platform Control (2026 forecast) maps to harnesses: Golden Paths, Guardrails, Safety Nets, Manual Review.

- Gap in coverage: Almost all harness content is engineer-to-engineer. Nobody is talking about who decides what the harness allows — the governance/ops layer. That's Jon's opening.
