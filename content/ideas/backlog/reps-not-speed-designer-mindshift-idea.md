## Core Insight

The designers thriving with AI aren't the ones who got faster - they're the ones who got comfortable being wrong on the first pass, trading the linear risk-reduction process for a reps-based iteration model.

## Key Concepts

- Speed is the wrong metric. Volume of iterations (reps) is what separates AI-native designers from AI-adjacent ones.
- Linear design processes exist to manage risk and protect the designer. AI makes early wrongness cheap. Designers who haven't made peace with that are still optimizing for the wrong thing.
- The psychological barrier is real and underappreciated. It's not a skill gap. It's a tolerance-for-imperfection gap.
- The Figma Make pilot at NYT surfaces this live: PMs and designers prototyping with AI diverge fast. Some ship 12 iterations by lunch. Others spend the same time perfecting their first prompt.
- "Reps" reframes the whole conversation - from AI as acceleration tool to AI as practice medium. You get better by being wrong faster, not by being right slower.

## Content Angles

**For designers:**

- The linear design process wasn't designed for speed. It was designed to protect you from looking wrong in front of stakeholders. AI changes the cost of wrongness. That's what you're actually adapting to, not the tool.
- Practical reframe: your job isn't to get the first draft right. Your job is to get to draft 8 fast enough that draft 8 is worth showing.

**For design managers and product leaders:**

- The designers struggling with AI aren't the ones who can't use the tools. They're the ones whose performance identity is still wrapped up in precision and craft on the first pass. That's a coaching conversation, not a training one.
- What you're actually building is a culture of fast imperfection, not a culture of AI tools.

**For engineers (relatable angle):**

- Engineers figured this out with version control. The cost of a bad commit dropped to near zero, so the iteration rate exploded. Designers are living through the same shift now. Figma Make is basically git for design thinking.

**For skeptics:**

- "But quality matters." Yes. And reps is how you get to quality faster. The designers cranking out 12 prototypes aren't shipping prototype 1. They're shipping prototype 12. That's the point.
